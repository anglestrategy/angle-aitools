# Cutting the AI Out of the Prose

## Why this exists

AI-written prose has a small set of tells that any creative or strategy lead will spot in three seconds. Strip them and the same content reads as if a person wrote it. Don't strip them and the work loses authority before the client has read a sentence.

This is a non-negotiable pass on every word that leaves the studio.

## The five tells

### 1. Em dashes (—)

The single biggest tell. AI overuses em dashes as a rhythm crutch. Replace each one with a period, a comma, parentheses, or a semicolon.

> AI: The park is not a teaser — it is a destination.
> Real: The park is not a teaser. It is a destination.

> AI: A platform that scales — across phases, audiences, and time.
> Real: A platform that scales across phases, audiences, and time.

### 2. Clipped contrast pairs ("Not X. Y.")

Two-sentence "not X, but Y" constructions read as ad copy. Use them once a section at most. Soften the rest by extending into longer sentences.

> AI: Not a campaign. A platform.
> Real: The work has to behave like a platform rather than a campaign.

### 3. Triadic rhythm

Three short noun phrases in a row ("Direct. Clear. Confident.") is a copywriter tic that AI imitates badly.

> AI: Direct. Clear. Confident.
> Real: The voice is direct and confident, and the structure stays clear.

### 4. Pitch-deck abstractions

A small vocabulary of words that signal "I am a presentation," not "I am a person." Avoid unless the client uses them.

Common offenders: *intake, diagnosis, frame, reads, guardrails, handoff, north star, lighthouse, unlock, leverage, double down, peer document, surface area, signal, lean in, sit underneath, the work the work refuses to be.*

See `references/words-and-patterns-to-cut.md` for substitutions.

### 5. Overlong dependent clauses

Three or more nested clauses in a sentence signal AI. Break into two sentences.

> AI: The platform should work across all three audience rings, for the brand, across every phase of the rollout, into the next chapters, and through the visit itself.
> Real: The platform should work across all three audience rings and every phase of the rollout. It also has to keep working when the next chapters of the project arrive.

## The pass

1. Read the prose aloud. If it sounds like a TED talk script, cut.
2. Search the document for `—`. Replace each one with the right punctuation for the sentence.
3. Search for short clipped sentences. Decide which ones to extend.
4. Run the vocabulary check against `references/words-and-patterns-to-cut.md`.
5. Read the prose aloud again.

## The test

Send a paragraph to a creative director with no context. If they ask "did AI write this?", you have not done the pass.

## When the tells are allowed

- The client's own brand voice uses them. (If the client deck is full of em dashes, match.)
- A single statement that genuinely needs the rhythm. (One per section at most.)
- A direct quote from somebody else.

Otherwise: cut.
