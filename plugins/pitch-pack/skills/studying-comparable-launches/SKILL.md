# Studying Comparable Launches

## What it is

Five to eight reference projects, each written up as two short paragraphs:

1. **How they positioned it.** What the launch communication actually said and how it framed the project.
2. **What we take from it.** The one move worth borrowing, written as a single takeaway for the creative team.

The point is to lift the communication move, not to summarise the project. Detailed case studies belong in academic research, not in a pitch.

## When to use

- Section seven of the Strategy Approach.
- A stand-alone Comparable Launches HTML deep-dive (when there are 6+ references and they each deserve their own panel).
- Background research the creative team can scan in five minutes.

## How to pick references

Look for projects that:

- Sit in the same category (park to park, museum to museum, residential to residential).
- Launched a single asset that had to do a big job (good analogue for a small slice carrying a big story).
- Are well-documented in trade press or design publications.
- Each demonstrate a *different* communication move. Variety beats density.

Avoid:

- Multiple projects that did the same thing. Pick one and move on.
- Projects that depended on a fluke (a single viral moment, a celebrity, a one-off media bet).
- Projects whose launch communication is hard to research from the outside.

## How to read a launch

For each reference, find and study:

- The launch press kit or first-week coverage.
- The visual identity (does the project have a logotype or does it lean on its host institution?).
- The programming or content calendar (was the launch a single moment or a rolling campaign?).
- The first three pieces of earned media (what did the press lead with?).

## Voice

Plain English. Short sentences. No design-school adjectives. Every reference paragraph is doing the same job: explain the move in one breath.

**Required pass before delivery:** run every word through `cutting-the-AI-out-of-the-prose`.
